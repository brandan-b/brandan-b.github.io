Design spotlight

Keeping the dragon phase hard but fair

The problem

The last phase has to be the hardest fight in the game. Morvath has his strongest attacks, and new whelps keep joining. Without care, that turns into chaos where the player can't tell what's hurting them or how close they are to winning.

The goals

  • Keep the win condition clear at all times.
  • Keep the biggest attacks readable.
  • Let the finish come down to skill, not RNG.

The solution

  • Four soulfire braziers give the phase a clear win condition the player can track.
  • Whelp reinforcements every 25s keep the pressure on early, then stop once three braziers are lit. Lighting the last brazier grounds Morvath for a short damage window, and fighting whelps during that window made the ending drag on and come down to RNG.
  • Deep Breath is a fly-by right over the player, so the most dangerous attack is always on screen.
  • The Soulfire timer is hidden in this phase, so one lit brazier never looks like enough.

The result

Playtesters across a range of play styles agreed the difficulty was right for a final boss. With no whelps in the final window, the finish rewards execution instead of RNG.

How the game flows
  • The Hatchery: the tutorial, where you learn to move, fight and cast.
  • The Sigil Halls: the hub that connects every area.
  • The Ritual Chamber and The Dragon Crypt: one seal each, in either order.
  • The Crucible: the third seal, which opens once you have the first two.
  • The Throne Room: the fight against Morvath, which needs all three seals.
The Morvath fight
  1. Whelp waves. Hold off waves of whelps before Morvath steps in.
  2. Human form. Morvath fights on foot and throws soulfire orbs.
  3. Transformation. He transforms into a dragon and heals.
  4. Dragon form. Light four soulfire braziers while he uses Deep Breath, swoops across the room and calls in new whelps every 25s. Once three braziers are lit, the whelps stop coming.
What playtesting changed

I judge every change by playing it. These are the problems testing turned up, and what I did about them.

The Soulfire timer misled players

ProblemIn the dragon phase, the timer made one lit brazier look like enough.

ChangeHid the timer for that phase, so the screen only shows what's true.

Deep Breath hid the boss

ProblemMoving Morvath closer for his breath attack felt wrong in play.

ChangeTurned it into a fly-by right over the player, so the biggest attack is always on screen.

Summoned whelps felt unfair

ProblemWhelps could shove the player around during the dragon phase.

ChangeRemoved the push. Whelps still pressure the player, but never take away control.

Enemies reset too hard

ProblemAfter a death in the Crucible, enemies snapped home and re-aggroed instantly.

ChangeThey now walk home at full health and only re-aggro within 4 tiles.

8-direction art felt clunky

ProblemI tried diagonal art for the dragon, but movement read worse in play.

ChangeKept four directions. Cleaner to read, and the fight felt better.

Leveling didn't scale

ProblemLeveling up after a set number of kills wouldn't hold up once I added more enemy types and areas.

ChangeSwitched to XP per enemy, so new enemies and areas slot in without rebalancing.

A stuck player had to restart

ProblemGetting caught on level geometry meant losing the whole run.

ChangeAdded an Unstuck button to the pause menu, with a cooldown so it can't be abused.

Controls should fit the player

ProblemFixed keys don't suit every player or keyboard layout.

ChangeAdded a Keybinds screen with a main and a backup key for every action.
